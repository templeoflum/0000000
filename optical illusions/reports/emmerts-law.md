## Abstract

Emmert's Law states that the apparent size of an afterimage is proportional to the perceived distance of the surface onto which it is projected. When an observer fixates a bright stimulus to generate a retinal afterimage and then views surfaces at different distances, the afterimage appears larger when projected onto more distant surfaces and smaller when projected onto nearer surfaces, despite maintaining a constant retinal size. Formally, S = k × D, where S is perceived size, D is perceived distance, and k is the constant visual angle subtended by the afterimage on the retina. This phenomenon demonstrates the operation of size constancy mechanisms that normally compensate for the inverse relationship between object distance and retinal image size. With afterimages, however, the retinal image remains fixed, so size constancy scaling produces illusory size changes. Emmert's Law provides strong evidence that perceived size is not determined solely by retinal image size but involves an interaction between angular size and depth information. The law has been used to investigate distance perception, size constancy development, and depth cue integration. It reveals fundamental principles about how the visual system infers object properties from ambiguous retinal input.
