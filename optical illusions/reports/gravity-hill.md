## Abstract

A gravity hill, also known as a magnetic hill or mystery hill, is a location where the surrounding landscape creates a powerful visual illusion that causes a downhill slope to appear as if it were an uphill slope, leading observers to perceive that objects are rolling or flowing uphill against gravity. This perceptual phenomenon occurs when the visible horizon is obscured or tilted in such a way that the observer's visual system misinterprets the true orientation of the ground surface, typically due to the configuration of surrounding terrain, trees, or other visual reference features that provide misleading cues about the true horizontal and vertical. When a vehicle is placed in neutral on what appears to be an upward slope at such a location, it will roll "uphill" in apparent defiance of gravity, creating a compelling but illusory demonstration of anti-gravitational forces. The illusion is strongest when the true horizon is hidden from view and the local visual context—such as sloping terrain on either side, tilted tree lines, or the angle of nearby roads—provides reference frames that conflict with the true gravitational vertical. Gravity hills have become popular tourist attractions in numerous locations worldwide, often accompanied by folklore attributing the effect to magnetic anomalies, supernatural forces, or mysterious energy fields, despite the entirely perceptual nature of the phenomenon.
