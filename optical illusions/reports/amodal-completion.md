## Abstract

Amodal completion refers to the perceptual phenomenon whereby observers automatically perceive complete object shapes and surfaces even when portions of those objects are physically occluded from view. Unlike modal completion, where illusory surfaces appear visible with specific brightness or color properties, amodal completion involves the perceptual inference of hidden structure without conscious visual experience of the occluded regions. When a circle is partially hidden behind a rectangle, for instance, observers spontaneously perceive a complete circular object continuing behind the occluder rather than a truncated arc abutting the rectangle's edge. This completion process operates automatically and is remarkably robust, occurring even when multiple alternative interpretations are geometrically possible. Amodal completion plays a fundamental role in object recognition and scene understanding, allowing the visual system to construct coherent representations of objects in cluttered environments where partial occlusion is the norm rather than the exception. The phenomenon has been extensively studied using both psychophysical methods and neuroimaging techniques, revealing involvement of mid-level visual areas and suggesting that completion processes reflect the visual system's implicit assumptions about object continuity, surface properties, and the physical constraints governing natural scenes.
