## Abstract

The green flash is a brief optical phenomenon in which a green spot or flash of light is visible at the uppermost rim of the sun for a duration of typically one to two seconds immediately after sunset or immediately before sunrise. This atmospheric effect occurs due to refraction of sunlight as it passes through the Earth's atmosphere, which acts as a weak prism that disperses the sun's light into its component colors, with different wavelengths being refracted by slightly different amounts. Under normal viewing conditions, the sun's brightness overwhelms the color separation and the disc appears white or yellow, but when the sun is at or just below the horizon, most of the disc is obscured and atmospheric refraction creates a situation where different colored images of the sun are vertically displaced from one another by small angles. Red light, being least refracted, appears lowest, while blue and violet light, being most refracted, would appear highest, but these shorter wavelengths are typically scattered away by atmospheric particles. Green light, being intermediate in wavelength and suffering less scattering than blue, becomes briefly visible as the last colored component to disappear at sunset or the first to appear at sunrise. The phenomenon requires exceptionally clear atmospheric conditions and a distant, unobstructed horizon, making it relatively rare and brief, though under optimal conditions it can last several seconds.
