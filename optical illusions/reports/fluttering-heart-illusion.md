## Abstract

The Fluttering Heart Illusion is a motion phenomenon in which an isoluminant colored shape, typically rendered in a heart configuration, appears to flutter, jitter, or oscillate when presented against a dark background as the observer's eyes move. The illusion is characterized by an apparent instability or trembling motion of the colored form, despite the fact that the stimulus itself remains completely stationary on the display. The effect is particularly pronounced when the colored shape is equiluminant or nearly equiluminant with the background, meaning that the two regions differ primarily in chromatic content rather than luminance. Eye movements, including microsaccades and smooth pursuit, appear to be critical for eliciting the fluttering percept, as the illusion is substantially reduced or eliminated during steady fixation. The phenomenon demonstrates interactions between chromatic processing, motion detection mechanisms, and eye movement signals in the visual system. The Fluttering Heart Illusion belongs to a broader class of illusory motion effects associated with isoluminant stimuli, revealing that chromatic information alone can generate spurious motion signals when combined with retinal image motion produced by eye movements. Understanding this illusion contributes to knowledge about how the visual system distinguishes self-generated motion from external object motion and how chromatic and achromatic pathways interact in motion perception.
