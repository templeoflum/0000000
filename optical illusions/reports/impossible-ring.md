## Abstract

The Impossible Ring is an impossible object depicting a torus or ring-like structure with a twist or other topological manipulation that creates contradictory depth relationships incompatible with three-dimensional Euclidean space. The figure typically shows a continuous band that loops back on itself to form a closed ring, but with depth cues suggesting that the band passes alternately in front of and behind itself in ways that violate topological constraints. Unlike a Möbius strip, which has a genuine topological twist that can exist in three dimensions, the Impossible Ring presents depth relationships that cannot be consistently maintained in any valid three-dimensional configuration. The illusion exploits the visual system's use of occlusion cues, where one surface passing in front of another indicates relative depth. By carefully arranging these occlusion relationships around the ring, the figure creates a scenario where following the band around its complete circuit requires accepting contradictory depth orderings. Each local region appears geometrically plausible with proper smooth curvature and consistent surface properties, but the global integration of depth cues produces an impossible structure. Variations of the Impossible Ring include designs with multiple bands, interlocking rings with paradoxical relationships, and rings that incorporate other impossible object elements such as Penrose triangle geometry. The figure demonstrates fundamental principles about how local perceptual cues are integrated into global three-dimensional representations and how carefully constructed two-dimensional projections can suggest impossible spatial configurations.
