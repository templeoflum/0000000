## Abstract

The Autokinetic Effect is a striking illusion of motion in which a small, stationary point of light viewed in complete darkness appears to move spontaneously and erratically. Despite the objective stability of the light source, observers consistently report perceiving the point drifting, wandering, or jumping in various directions, with individual reports showing considerable variability in the perceived trajectory, speed, and extent of movement. The effect emerges after several seconds of fixation and can persist for extended viewing periods, with the illusory motion patterns changing unpredictably over time. The phenomenon is highly susceptible to cognitive and social influences, including suggestion and group conformity effects, making it a classic demonstration in social psychology as well as visual perception research. The autokinetic effect has been attributed to various factors including involuntary eye movements, the absence of stable reference points in darkness, neural noise in motion detection systems, and the visual system's tendency to interpret ambiguous sensory input as motion. The illusion provides insight into the role of spatial reference frames in motion perception and demonstrates how the visual system responds when deprived of the contextual information normally available for stabilizing perception.
