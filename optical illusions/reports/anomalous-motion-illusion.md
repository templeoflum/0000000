## Abstract

The Anomalous Motion Illusion refers to the compelling perception of motion in objectively static visual patterns. This phenomenon typically arises from repeated asymmetric luminance profiles arranged in specific spatial configurations, where gradients of brightness or contrast create the impression of continuous movement despite the absence of any physical displacement. The illusion is particularly robust when viewing patterns composed of sawtooth or stepped luminance transitions, with the direction of perceived motion corresponding to the orientation of these gradients. Observers report experiencing smooth, continuous motion that can persist for extended viewing periods, though the effect often diminishes with prolonged fixation. The strength of the illusion varies with factors including spatial frequency, contrast level, and viewing distance. Eye movements and peripheral viewing conditions can enhance the effect, suggesting interactions between low-level motion detection mechanisms and higher-level perceptual processes. The anomalous motion phenomenon has become a valuable tool for investigating the computational principles underlying visual motion perception, particularly the mechanisms that extract motion signals from spatial patterns of luminance change.
