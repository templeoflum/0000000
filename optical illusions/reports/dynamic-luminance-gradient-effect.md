## Abstract

The Dynamic Luminance-Gradient Effect is a temporal variant of classical luminance gradient illusions in which smoothly varying changes in luminance gradients over time produce enhanced or novel lightness perception effects. While static luminance gradients have long been known to influence lightness perception through mechanisms related to spatial filtering and edge detection, the dynamic version introduces temporal modulation that can amplify illusory effects or produce qualitatively different perceptual experiences. In typical demonstrations, regions that gradually transition from containing one gradient profile to another can produce enhanced simultaneous contrast effects, apparent motion of lightness boundaries, or bistable perceptual states where the same physical stimulus yields alternating interpretations. The dynamic nature of these stimuli engages both spatial and temporal processing mechanisms in the visual system, potentially involving temporal filtering operations, adaptation to temporal frequency content, and interactions between sustained and transient neural channels. Dynamic luminance gradients may be particularly effective at revealing the time course of lightness computation processes, including the temporal dynamics of filling-in mechanisms and the integration time windows over which edge information is processed to determine surface lightness. This class of illusions has received less systematic investigation than static gradient effects but offers valuable insights into the temporal aspects of mid-level visual processing and lightness perception.
