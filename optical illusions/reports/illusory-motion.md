## Abstract

Illusory Motion refers to a broad class of visual phenomena in which observers perceive motion in stimuli that are objectively static or stationary. These illusions encompass diverse perceptual experiences ranging from subtle drift or shimmer to vivid rotation, expansion, or wave-like propagation, all arising from patterns that contain no physical movement. Illusory motion effects can be induced through various stimulus manipulations including specific arrangements of luminance gradients, color contrasts, spatial frequencies, and orientations. The phenomenology varies considerably across different illusory motion configurations, but common features include dependence on eye movements, sensitivity to attention and fixation patterns, and modulation by stimulus contrast and temporal viewing duration. Major subcategories include peripheral drift illusions, anomalous motion illusions, and motion-from-form effects. The mechanisms underlying illusory motion are thought to involve inappropriate activation of motion-sensitive neurons in early and mid-level visual areas, interactions between spatial filtering operations and temporal signals from eye movements, and neural adaptation effects that create transient imbalances in motion opponent systems. Illusory motion phenomena have proven valuable for investigating the computational principles of motion detection, revealing how the visual system can generate motion signals from purely spatial information under certain conditions. These illusions also demonstrate the constructive nature of perception and the complexity of distinguishing self-generated from external motion.
