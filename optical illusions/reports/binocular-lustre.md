## Abstract

Binocular lustre is a perceptual phenomenon in which presenting different luminance levels to the two eyes produces the appearance of a lustrous, shiny, or metallic surface quality that cannot be achieved through monocular viewing alone. When one eye views a lighter region while the other eye simultaneously views a darker region of the same spatial location, observers report a distinctive glossy or luminous surface appearance rather than simple binocular rivalry or fusion to an intermediate brightness. This effect demonstrates that the visual system uses interocular luminance differences as a cue to surface properties, particularly specularity and reflectance characteristics. The phenomenon has been documented since the 19th century but remains relatively understudied compared to other binocular phenomena. Binocular lustre appears to involve specialized mechanisms for integrating conflicting monocular signals into coherent surface property representations. The effect is sensitive to the magnitude of interocular luminance difference, spatial configuration, and temporal dynamics. Understanding binocular lustre provides insights into how the brain infers material properties from binocular disparity and luminance cues, with implications for stereoscopic display technology and virtual reality applications.
