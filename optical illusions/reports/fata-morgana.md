## Abstract

Fata Morgana is a complex and elaborate form of superior mirage in which distant objects appear dramatically distorted, often stretched vertically, inverted, or multiplied into stacked images that can resemble castles, towers, or fantastical structures. Named after Morgan le Fay, the sorceress from Arthurian legend who was said to create illusions to lure sailors to their doom, this phenomenon occurs when multiple layers of air at different temperatures create multiple refracting surfaces that bend light rays in complex paths. Unlike simple mirages that produce a single inverted or displaced image, Fata Morgana mirages involve thermal inversion layers that create multiple alternating upright and inverted images, often compressed or stretched in the vertical dimension, producing bizarre and rapidly changing distortions. The effect is most commonly observed over large bodies of water or flat land surfaces where strong temperature gradients can develop, particularly in polar regions, deserts, or over cold ocean surfaces. Objects appearing in a Fata Morgana display can seem to tower impossibly high, appear segmented or stacked, or morph rapidly as atmospheric conditions fluctuate. The phenomenon has been credited with inspiring numerous historical accounts of phantom islands, floating cities, and supernatural visions, as the distorted images bear little resemblance to their source objects and can appear and disappear within minutes.
