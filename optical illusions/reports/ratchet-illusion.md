## Abstract

The Ratchet Illusion is a motion perception phenomenon in which a continuously rotating pattern appears to move in discrete, stepwise increments rather than smoothly. This illusory perception of quantized motion occurs despite the physical stimulus undergoing constant angular velocity rotation. The illusion is typically observed with specific spatial patterns, such as radial gratings or circular arrays of elements, that contain strong periodic structure. The stepwise appearance may reflect temporal sampling characteristics of motion detection mechanisms in the visual system, or interactions between motion energy detectors tuned to different spatial and temporal frequencies. The Ratchet Illusion demonstrates that smooth physical motion does not always produce smooth perceived motion, and that the visual system's representation of continuous motion can be disrupted by particular combinations of spatial pattern and rotation speed. Understanding this illusion contributes to broader knowledge about how motion detection mechanisms integrate information over space and time, and reveals constraints on the temporal resolution of motion perception.
