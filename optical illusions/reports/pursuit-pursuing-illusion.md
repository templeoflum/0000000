## Abstract

The Pursuit-Pursuing Illusion is a perceptual phenomenon in which the motion characteristics of a target object appear distorted during smooth pursuit eye movements. When observers track a moving target with their eyes, the perceived velocity, trajectory, or continuity of the target's motion can differ systematically from its actual physical motion. This illusion arises from the complex interaction between retinal motion signals and extraretinal signals related to eye movement commands. During smooth pursuit, the visual system must distinguish between retinal image motion caused by object motion in the world and retinal image motion caused by the observer's own eye movements. The Pursuit-Pursuing Illusion demonstrates that this compensation mechanism is imperfect under certain conditions, leading to predictable misperceptions. The phenomenon has implications for understanding how the visual system integrates multiple sources of information to construct a stable and accurate representation of motion in the environment, and highlights the computational challenges involved in maintaining perceptual constancy during active vision.
