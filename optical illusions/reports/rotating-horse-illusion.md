## Abstract

The Rotating Horse Illusion is a bistable motion perception phenomenon in which a silhouette of a horse in rotation appears to spontaneously reverse its direction of rotation. The ambiguity arises because the two-dimensional silhouette projection is compatible with two different three-dimensional interpretations of the horse's spatial orientation and rotation direction. Without depth cues such as shading, occlusion, or binocular disparity, the visual system cannot unambiguously determine whether the horse is rotating clockwise or counterclockwise, leading to perceptual alternations between the two interpretations. This illusion is structurally similar to other bistable motion stimuli such as the spinning dancer illusion and rotating mask illusion. The phenomenon demonstrates that motion perception is not purely stimulus-driven but involves high-level interpretive processes that resolve ambiguity through inference about three-dimensional structure. The spontaneous perceptual reversals reflect stochastic dynamics in neural populations representing competing motion interpretations, and the illusion provides a useful paradigm for studying the neural basis of perceptual bistability and decision-making in the motion domain.
