## Abstract

The Infinity Mirror Illusion is a visual phenomenon created by positioning two parallel mirrors facing each other, resulting in the perception of an infinitely extending tunnel of reflections. When an object or light source is placed between the mirrors, viewers observe a seemingly endless corridor of progressively smaller, fainter duplicates receding into depth. This illusion exploits the visual system's interpretation of size gradients and luminance decay as depth cues, triggering perceptual mechanisms normally used to estimate distance in natural environments. The effect is particularly compelling when enhanced with internal lighting, such as LED strips, which create discrete points of light that appear to extend to an unreachable vanishing point. The infinity mirror demonstrates how the brain constructs three-dimensional spatial representations from two-dimensional retinal information, even when the implied depth vastly exceeds the physical space available. This phenomenon has found widespread application in art installations, interior design, and theatrical staging, where it creates dramatic spatial expansions within confined areas. The illusion reveals fundamental principles of depth perception, including the role of perspective convergence, texture gradients, and atmospheric perspective in constructing our experience of visual space.
