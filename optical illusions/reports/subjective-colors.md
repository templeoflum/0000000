## Abstract

Subjective colors refer to a diverse set of chromatic percepts that arise from physically achromatic stimuli through various temporal, spatial, or configurational manipulations. Unlike veridical color perception, which results from differential activation of cone photoreceptors by varying wavelength compositions, subjective colors emerge through neural interactions that occur when non-chromatic patterns are presented under specific conditions. Major categories include temporal subjective colors such as Fechner colors (perceived during flicker of black-and-white patterns), Benham's top (concentric arcs appearing colored during rotation), and sequential metacontrast; spatial subjective colors arising from specific geometric arrangements; and those induced by eye movements across stationary patterns. The percepts are typically desaturated compared to spectral colors and vary considerably across observers. Theoretical explanations invoke differential latencies in neural pathways processing luminance increments versus decrements, temporal phase differences between chromatic channels, interactions between magnocellular and parvocellular streams, and transient neural responses to rapid luminance changes. Subjective colors provide critical constraints for models of temporal processing in color vision and demonstrate that chromatic experience can be dissociated from wavelength-selective photoreceptor activation. The phenomena highlight the constructive and dynamic nature of color perception.
