## Abstract

Lenticular printing is a technology that produces images with an illusion of depth or the ability to change appearance when viewed from different angles, achieved by printing interlaced images behind an array of cylindrical lenses (lenticules) that selectively direct different image strips to each eye or to viewers at different positions. The technique can create effects including stereoscopic depth perception, flip images that alternate between two or more distinct pictures, animation sequences that appear to move as viewing angle changes, and zoom effects where portions of the image appear to magnify. Lenticular printing exploits the geometric optics of lens arrays to control which image information reaches the observer as a function of viewing position, effectively multiplexing multiple images into a single physical substrate. The perceptual effects depend on precise alignment between the printed image and the lens array, with the lenticule pitch determining the viewing angle range and the number of interleavable frames. The technology demonstrates principles of spatial filtering, as the lens array acts as a directional filter for the underlying image, as well as depth perception mechanisms when different images are presented to the two eyes. Lenticular prints have been widely used in advertising, packaging, collectible cards, and decorative applications, providing demonstrations of viewpoint-dependent image transformations without requiring electronic displays or special viewing devices.
