## Abstract

Sigma Motion is a type of apparent motion phenomenon that occurs at specific spatiotemporal parameter ranges, distinguished from other forms of apparent motion such as beta movement or phi phenomenon. The term refers to motion perception elicited by sequential presentation of discrete stimuli at particular combinations of spatial separation and temporal interval. Sigma motion is characterized by specific perceptual qualities that differ from optimal apparent motion, and it occupies a distinct region in the spatiotemporal parameter space of apparent motion stimuli. The phenomenon demonstrates that apparent motion is not a unitary percept but rather encompasses multiple qualitatively distinct motion experiences that emerge at different stimulus parameter ranges. Understanding sigma motion contributes to broader theories of apparent motion by revealing that the visual system employs different motion correspondence and interpolation mechanisms depending on the spatial and temporal characteristics of the input. The existence of sigma motion as a distinct category highlights the complexity of the motion integration processes that construct smooth motion percepts from discrete spatiotemporal samples.
