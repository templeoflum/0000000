## Abstract

The Scintillating Grid Illusion is a dynamic brightness illusion in which white dots located at the intersections of gray lines on a dark background appear to flicker or scintillate between bright and dark as observers move their gaze across the pattern. The illusory dark spots appear transiently at intersections in peripheral vision, but disappear when directly fixated. Discovered by Elke Lingelbach in 1994, this illusion represents a compelling variant of the classical Hermann Grid illusion, with the addition of white dots at intersections producing a dramatically enhanced dynamic effect. The scintillation occurs because the dots fall within the receptive fields of neurons with center-surround organization, and the specific geometry of the grid intersections creates spatial luminance patterns that strongly modulate these neurons' responses. The illusion is particularly sensitive to the relative widths of the lines and dots, the spacing of the grid, and the luminance relationships between the components. Eye movements and attentional shifts cause the pattern of neural activation to change dynamically, producing the characteristic scintillating appearance. The phenomenon demonstrates how the spatial filtering properties of early visual processing, combined with the dynamics of eye movements and peripheral versus foveal vision, can produce striking illusory percepts that are not present in the physical stimulus.
