## Abstract

The Luminance Gradient Illusion refers to a class of perceptual phenomena in which physically uniform surfaces appear to contain luminance gradients, or conversely, surfaces containing actual gradients appear more uniform than their physical properties would suggest. These effects arise when target surfaces are positioned adjacent to or surrounded by regions containing luminance gradients in specific spatial configurations. The illusion demonstrates that the visual system's interpretation of shading and illumination patterns can induce complementary or assimilating gradient percepts in neighboring regions. For example, a uniform gray patch adjacent to a gradient may appear to contain a gradient in the opposite direction, as if the visual system is compensating for assumed illumination patterns. This phenomenon reveals fundamental aspects of how the visual system decomposes retinal luminance patterns into separate estimates of surface reflectance and illumination geometry. The strength of the illusory gradient depends on factors including the steepness of adjacent gradients, spatial frequency relationships, and the presence of explicit boundaries. The Luminance Gradient Illusion has implications for understanding lightness constancy mechanisms and provides constraints on computational models of intrinsic image decomposition. The effect highlights that perceived surface properties emerge from contextual analysis of extended spatial patterns rather than point-by-point luminance registration.
