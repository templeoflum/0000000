## Abstract

The Lilac Chaser, created by Jeremy Hinton in 2005, is a striking demonstration that combines several perceptual phenomena including motion perception, afterimages, and perceptual filling-in. The illusion consists of twelve lilac or magenta-colored discs arranged in a circle around a central fixation point. When observers fixate steadily on the central point, the discs sequentially disappear one at a time in a clockwise or counterclockwise pattern, creating a gap that rotates around the circle. After several seconds of viewing, observers typically report three distinct perceptual effects. First, the lilac discs gradually fade from view due to neural adaptation and Troxler fading. Second, a green disc appears to chase around the circle, filling the gap where each lilac disc disappears. This green afterimage arises from chromatic adaptation to the surrounding lilac discs, producing a complementary color percept. Third, observers may perceive the green disc as actively moving or jumping around the circle, following the rotating gap. The Lilac Chaser elegantly demonstrates how adaptation, afterimages, and motion perception interact in the visual system, revealing principles of neural fatigue, opponent color processing, and apparent motion. The illusion has become popular for educational demonstrations and illustrates how prolonged fixation can dramatically alter perceptual experience.
