## Abstract

The Reverse Spokes Illusion is a motion perception phenomenon in which the spokes of a rotating wheel appear to reverse their direction of rotation under certain viewing conditions. This illusion is related to temporal aliasing effects that occur when the spatiotemporal characteristics of the stimulus interact with the sampling properties of motion detection mechanisms in the visual system. The reversal typically occurs at specific rotation speeds where the temporal frequency of spoke presentation approaches or exceeds critical values for motion detection. Unlike the wagon wheel effect, which is often attributed to discrete temporal sampling in filmed or artificially illuminated displays, the Reverse Spokes Illusion can occur under continuous viewing conditions, suggesting contributions from neural temporal filtering rather than purely physical sampling artifacts. The phenomenon demonstrates that the perceived direction of rotation is not always veridically determined by physical motion direction, and that motion direction perception depends critically on the match between stimulus spatiotemporal parameters and the tuning characteristics of motion-sensitive neural mechanisms.
