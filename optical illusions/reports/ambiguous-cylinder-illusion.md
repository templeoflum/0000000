## Abstract

The Ambiguous Cylinder Illusion, created by mathematician and artist Kokichi Sugihara, is a striking three-dimensional optical illusion in which specially constructed physical objects appear to have one shape when viewed directly but appear as a completely different shape when viewed through a mirror reflection. The most famous version features objects that appear as circular cylinders when viewed directly but transform into square columns when seen in a mirror, or vice versa. This seemingly impossible transformation is achieved through careful construction of three-dimensional forms with cross-sections that are highly asymmetric and precisely calculated to produce different silhouettes from different viewing angles. The illusion exploits the visual system's tendency to make assumptions about object symmetry and viewing perspective, combined with the fact that mirrors reverse the viewing angle while the brain continues to apply the same depth interpretation heuristics. Sugihara's work has won international acclaim, including first prize at the Best Illusion of the Year Contest, and demonstrates fundamental principles about how the brain reconstructs three-dimensional structure from two-dimensional retinal images. The illusion highlights the inherent ambiguity in depth perception and the powerful role of Bayesian prior assumptions in visual processing.
