## Abstract

Delta Motion is a specific type of apparent motion phenomenon that occurs under particular temporal and spatial parameters, distinguished from other forms of apparent motion by its characteristic perceptual qualities and the stimulus conditions that produce it. While apparent motion in general refers to the perception of movement from sequentially presented static stimuli, delta motion represents a particular regime within the broader parameter space of apparent motion phenomena. The effect is observed when the timing and spacing of stimulus presentations fall within specific ranges that differ from those producing optimal beta motion or other apparent motion variants. Historical investigations of apparent motion distinguished multiple phenomenological categories based on interstimulus intervals and spatial separations, with delta motion occupying a specific position in this taxonomy. The perceptual experience of delta motion may differ qualitatively from smooth beta motion, potentially involving more discrete or jump-like motion percepts. Understanding delta motion contributes to a comprehensive picture of how the visual system processes temporal sequences of stimuli and constructs motion percepts across different spatiotemporal scales. The phenomenon highlights the parametric sensitivity of motion perception mechanisms and demonstrates that not all sequential stimulus presentations produce identical perceptual outcomes.
