## Abstract

The Barberpole Illusion is a compelling motion perception phenomenon in which diagonal stripes moving behind a rectangular aperture appear to move along the longer axis of the aperture rather than in their true direction of motion. Named after the traditional rotating barbershop pole that displays this effect, the illusion reveals fundamental principles about how the visual system integrates local motion signals into global motion percepts. When diagonal stripes move at an oblique angle behind a vertically oriented rectangular window, observers perceive predominantly vertical motion rather than the actual diagonal trajectory. The illusion arises from the aperture problem, a fundamental constraint in visual motion processing where the true direction of a contour can only be determined at terminators or endpoints. Local motion detectors along the interior of each stripe can only signal motion perpendicular to the stripe orientation, while the aperture boundaries provide additional motion signals that bias the perceived direction. The visual system resolves this ambiguity by integrating motion signals across the stimulus, with terminator signals at the aperture edges often dominating the final percept. The barberpole illusion has been instrumental in developing computational models of motion integration and understanding the hierarchical processing of motion information in visual cortex.
