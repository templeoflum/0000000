## Abstract

Perceptual Rivalry refers to the general phenomenon in which incompatible perceptual interpretations compete for conscious awareness, resulting in alternating dominance of mutually exclusive percepts despite constant sensory input. This umbrella category encompasses multiple specific forms of rivalry, including binocular rivalry (different images presented to each eye), ambiguous figures (single images admitting multiple interpretations), and motion rivalry (conflicting motion signals). The hallmark of perceptual rivalry is spontaneous, stochastic alternation between competing percepts, with perception fluctuating even when the physical stimulus remains unchanged. Rivalry phenomena provide a unique experimental window into the neural mechanisms of consciousness, perceptual selection, and attentional control, as the dissociation between stable sensory input and changing perceptual experience allows researchers to isolate neural correlates of subjective awareness. Theoretical accounts of rivalry invoke neural adaptation, reciprocal inhibition, attentional modulation, and hierarchical competition at multiple levels of visual processing. The dynamics of rivalry alternations exhibit characteristic statistical properties, including gamma-distributed dominance durations and traveling waves of perceptual dominance across the visual field. Perceptual rivalry has become a central paradigm in consciousness research, revealing fundamental principles about how the brain resolves ambiguity and constructs coherent perceptual interpretations from inherently ambiguous sensory data.
