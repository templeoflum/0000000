## Abstract

Heiligenschein, German for "holy light" or "halo," is an atmospheric optical phenomenon appearing as a bright, diffuse glow surrounding the shadow of the observer's head when cast upon certain surfaces, most commonly dew-covered grass or other vegetation. The effect results from retroreflection, wherein light is preferentially scattered back toward its source by spherical water droplets acting as crude lens systems. Each dewdrop focuses incoming sunlight onto the surface behind it, which then reflects the light back through the droplet, creating a focused return beam directed toward the light source and observer. This retroreflective enhancement is strongest along the antisolar direction, which coincides with the observer's shadow, producing the characteristic bright glow around the shadow of the observer's head while shadows of other objects or other observers' shadows appear normal. The phenomenon is distinct from the glory, another backscattering effect requiring different geometry and occurring with water droplets in the air rather than on surfaces. Heiligenschein is most readily observed in early morning when dew is abundant and the sun is at a low angle, though it can also occur on other retroreflective surfaces. The effect demonstrates principles of geometrical optics and has contributed to understanding of natural retroreflection mechanisms, with applications in retroreflective material design.
