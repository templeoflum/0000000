## Abstract

Figure-Ground Ambigrams are sophisticated visual designs in which both the figure (foreground) and ground (background) regions contain meaningful content, typically text or recognizable shapes, with different interpretations emerging depending on which region is assigned as figure versus ground. Unlike simple ambigrams that can be read in multiple orientations, figure-ground ambigrams exploit the fundamental perceptual process of figure-ground segregation to create designs where the negative space is as semantically meaningful as the positive space. Classic examples include designs where one word appears in the black regions while a different word or image appears in the white regions, often with the two meanings being semantically related or contrasting. This art form demonstrates the Gestalt principle that figure-ground assignment is an active, constructive process rather than a passive feature of the stimulus itself. The creation of effective figure-ground ambigrams requires careful attention to contour ownership, closure, symmetry, and other organizational principles that the visual system uses to determine which regions belong to objects and which constitute background. These designs have applications in logo design, typography, and art, and provide insight into the computational challenges the brain faces in parsing visual scenes into objects and their surroundings.
