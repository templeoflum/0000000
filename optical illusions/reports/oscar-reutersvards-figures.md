## Abstract

Oscar Reutersvärd's Figures represent a pioneering body of impossible object art created by Swedish artist Oscar Reutersvärd beginning in 1934, predating the more widely known impossible figures of the Penrose family by over two decades. Reutersvärd created his first impossible triangle construction at age 18, initiating a lifelong artistic exploration that ultimately produced over 2,500 impossible figure drawings. His works typically feature geometric constructions built from isometric cube elements arranged to create paradoxical three-dimensional configurations. The figures exploit depth cue conflicts where local geometric relationships appear consistent with normal three-dimensional space, but global integration reveals impossibilities. Reutersvärd's characteristic approach involved building complex impossible structures from simple cubic building blocks arranged on isometric grids, creating figures where edges and faces connect in locally plausible but globally inconsistent ways. His most famous creation, an impossible triangle composed of nine cubes arranged in three bars of three cubes each, demonstrates how apparently straightforward geometric elements can be configured to produce spatial paradoxes. The visual system interprets each cubic element and its local connections as depicting normal three-dimensional relationships, but following the structure around its complete circuit reveals contradictory depth orderings. Reutersvärd's extensive exploration of impossible geometry established fundamental principles later studied in perceptual psychology and influenced subsequent artists including M.C. Escher. His work demonstrates the rich variety of impossible configurations achievable through systematic manipulation of depth cues in two-dimensional representations.
