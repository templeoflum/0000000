## Abstract

The Stepping Feet Illusion, discovered by Stuart Anstis in 2001, is a compelling motion illusion in which two bars moving at constant and identical speeds appear to move at different velocities, creating the impression of alternating acceleration and deceleration reminiscent of stepping feet. The illusion is typically created by overlaying two horizontal bars on a background of alternating dark and light vertical stripes, with the bars positioned such that they alternately pass over contrasting background regions. When one bar is on a dark stripe while the other is on a light stripe, they appear to be moving at different speeds, even though both maintain constant velocity. The illusion demonstrates that perceived velocity is not solely determined by retinal motion signals but is strongly modulated by spatial context and contrast relationships. The phenomenon has been explained in terms of feature tracking mechanisms that are disrupted when moving objects have low contrast with their local background, or in terms of differential temporal processing of high-contrast versus low-contrast motion signals. The Stepping Feet Illusion provides important insights into how the visual system integrates spatial and temporal information to construct velocity estimates.
