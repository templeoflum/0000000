## Abstract

Gamma Motion refers to an illusory expansion or contraction perceived when a visual stimulus undergoes an abrupt change in its presence. Specifically, when a stimulus suddenly appears (onset), observers often report a brief perception of outward expansion or looming, as though the object is growing larger or moving toward the viewer. Conversely, when a stimulus suddenly disappears (offset), observers may perceive a transient inward contraction or receding motion. These illusory motion percepts occur despite no actual change in the stimulus size or position beyond its appearance or disappearance. Gamma Motion belongs to a historical taxonomy of motion phenomena that includes beta motion (apparent motion between discrete stimuli) and alpha motion (perceived movement of a single stimulus). The effect demonstrates that the visual system interprets sudden temporal transients not merely as changes in presence but as potential indicators of motion in depth or size change. The neural mechanisms underlying Gamma Motion likely involve interactions between transient channels sensitive to abrupt luminance changes and motion-selective neurons that respond to expansion or contraction patterns. Understanding Gamma Motion contributes to broader knowledge about how the visual system infers three-dimensional motion and depth from two-dimensional retinal signals, and how onset and offset transients are processed differently from sustained stimulus presence.
