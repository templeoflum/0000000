## Abstract

The Brocken Spectre is a dramatic atmospheric optical phenomenon in which an observer's shadow is cast onto clouds or fog below them, appearing as a magnified, often gigantic figure surrounded by colored rings of light known as a glory. Named after the Brocken, the highest peak in Germany's Harz Mountains where the phenomenon was frequently observed and documented, this effect occurs when the sun is positioned behind an observer who is standing above a cloud layer or fog bank. The observer's shadow is projected onto the cloud surface, but the apparent magnification of the shadow is primarily a perceptual illusion rather than a true optical magnification: the shadow appears enormous because the observer lacks reliable distance cues to the cloud surface and applies size constancy scaling inappropriately, interpreting the nearby shadow as being much larger than it actually is. The Brocken Spectre is frequently accompanied by a glory—concentric colored rings produced by backscattering and diffraction of sunlight by cloud droplets—which appears centered on the observer's shadow and enhances the supernatural or otherworldly appearance of the phenomenon. This combination of shadow projection and colored rings has inspired numerous legends and supernatural interpretations throughout history, particularly in mountainous regions where the effect is most commonly observed.
