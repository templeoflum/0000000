## Abstract

The Profile or Portrait Illusion is an ambiguous figure that alternates between appearing as a face viewed from the side (profile view) and a face viewed from the front (portrait or frontal view). This bistable stimulus demonstrates how identical contour information can be organized by the visual system into fundamentally different three-dimensional facial interpretations. The perceptual switch involves a reorganization of which features correspond to which facial landmarks, with lines and curves taking on different anatomical significance depending on the dominant interpretation. When perceived as a profile, certain contours define the outline of a face in side view, while the same contours are reinterpreted as internal facial features or boundaries when the percept switches to a frontal view. The illusion exemplifies the constructive nature of face perception and the role of viewpoint-dependent processing in facial recognition. Spontaneous alternations between the two interpretations occur during sustained viewing, reflecting neural competition between alternative perceptual hypotheses. Research using this and similar view-dependent ambiguous figures has provided insights into how the visual system represents faces across different viewpoints, the automaticity of face detection mechanisms, and the dynamics of perceptual bistability in socially relevant stimuli. The illusion highlights the interpretive flexibility of the face processing system when confronted with ambiguous input.
