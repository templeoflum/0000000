## Abstract

The Koffka Ring Illusion consists of a uniform gray ring that spans two regions of different luminance, typically a dark and light background. Despite the ring's physical uniformity, observers perceive a striking discontinuity in its lightness at the boundary between the two backgrounds. The portion of the ring on the dark background appears lighter than the portion on the light background, even though both segments have identical luminance. This illusion challenges simple contrast theories that predict lightness based solely on local luminance ratios. The phenomenon demonstrates that the visual system does not process lightness independently at each point, but rather integrates information across the entire stimulus configuration. The Koffka Ring has been central to debates about whether lightness perception is primarily determined by local contrast, edge integration, or surface decomposition processes. The illusion reveals the complexity of belongingness and grouping in lightness perception: the ring is simultaneously grouped as a single object (favoring uniform appearance) and segregated by the background boundary (favoring contrast effects). The relative strength of these competing processes determines the magnitude of the perceived discontinuity, which varies with factors including ring width, background luminance ratio, and viewing conditions.
