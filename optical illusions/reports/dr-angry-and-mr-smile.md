## Abstract

Dr. Angry and Mr. Smile is a facial expression illusion in which two face images appear to swap emotional expressions when viewed from different distances. The effect is created by combining images of two faces with opposite expressions using spatial frequency filtering: one face's angry expression is encoded in high spatial frequencies while a smiling expression is encoded in low spatial frequencies, and vice versa for the second face. When viewed up close, observers perceive the high-frequency details and see one emotional configuration. When the same images are viewed from several feet away, the high frequencies become less visible and the low-frequency information dominates, revealing the opposite emotional expressions. This illusion demonstrates the distance-dependent nature of spatial frequency processing and the critical role of different frequency bands in face perception. The effect is related to hybrid image techniques and reveals that facial expression recognition relies on scale-dependent visual information. Low spatial frequencies may carry coarse emotional signals processed rapidly and unconsciously, while high frequencies provide fine detail for conscious expression analysis. The illusion highlights the hierarchical and multi-scale nature of face processing in the visual system.
