## Abstract

The All The Same Color Illusion demonstrates that physically identical regions can appear dramatically different in color or lightness depending on their surrounding context. This phenomenon occurs when surfaces that share identical spectral reflectance properties are embedded in different chromatic or luminance environments, leading to substantial perceptual discrepancies. The illusion powerfully illustrates the visual system's tendency to compute surface properties relative to their local context rather than encoding absolute physical values. This contextual modulation reflects the fundamental computational challenge of color and lightness constancy: the visual system must infer stable surface properties from retinal images that vary with illumination conditions. The effect demonstrates that perception prioritizes ecological validity over physical accuracy, interpreting identical sensory inputs differently when contextual cues suggest different surface properties or lighting conditions. The illusion is particularly compelling in demonstrations where viewers struggle to believe that two regions are genuinely identical until they are spatially connected or measured. This perceptual phenomenon provides critical insights into mid-level visual processing mechanisms responsible for computing surface reflectance from luminance patterns, highlighting the constructive nature of visual perception.
