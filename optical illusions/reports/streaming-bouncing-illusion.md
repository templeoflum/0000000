## Abstract

The Streaming/Bouncing Illusion is a bistable motion perception phenomenon in which two identical objects moving toward each other, passing through the same spatial location, and then moving apart can be perceived in two qualitatively different ways: either as streaming through each other without interaction, or as bouncing off each other upon contact. The visual stimulus alone is inherently ambiguous and compatible with both interpretations. Remarkably, the perceptual interpretation can be strongly biased by cross-modal factors, particularly the presentation of a brief auditory stimulus synchronized with the moment when the objects overlap spatially. When a transient sound occurs at the moment of coincidence, observers are significantly more likely to perceive the objects as bouncing rather than streaming. This illusion demonstrates that motion trajectory interpretation involves high-level inference processes that integrate information across sensory modalities, and that auditory information can influence fundamental aspects of visual motion perception. The phenomenon provides important evidence for multisensory integration in motion perception and highlights the role of causal inference in resolving ambiguous motion correspondence problems.
