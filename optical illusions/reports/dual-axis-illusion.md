## Abstract

The Dual-Axis Illusion is a bistable or multistable motion perception phenomenon in which a rotating three-dimensional object appears to spin around two different axes either simultaneously or in alternation, despite having only a single physical axis of rotation. This illusion typically occurs when viewing ambiguous motion displays, such as silhouettes or wireframe objects, where depth information is absent or impoverished, leaving the rotation axis underspecified. Observers report spontaneous perceptual switches between different interpretations of the rotation axis, with periods of stable perception alternating with sudden transitions between distinct rotational configurations. The phenomenon demonstrates the visual system's capacity to generate multiple equally plausible interpretations of ambiguous motion patterns and highlights the role of top-down inference in motion perception. Factors influencing the perceptual interpretation include the initial viewing conditions, attentional state, prior expectations, and individual differences in perceptual biases. The illusion is related to structure-from-motion processes, where the visual system attempts to recover three-dimensional structure from two-dimensional motion patterns. Neural competition between alternative interpretations, similar to mechanisms underlying other bistable phenomena, likely contributes to the perceptual alternations. The dual-axis illusion provides insights into motion perception under ambiguity and the constructive processes underlying three-dimensional motion interpretation.
