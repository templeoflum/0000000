## Abstract

The Drifting Grating Illusion encompasses a class of systematic misperceptions that occur when viewing moving periodic patterns, particularly sinusoidal or square-wave gratings in motion. These illusions manifest as errors in the perceived direction, speed, or quality of motion compared to the physical properties of the drifting stimulus. Observers may experience biases in drift direction, particularly when gratings are viewed through apertures or combined with other visual features, or may perceive non-uniform motion speeds across different regions of the visual field despite uniform physical drift rates. The illusions can also involve perceived changes in spatial frequency or orientation that accompany the motion. Such effects arise from the computational strategies the visual system employs to extract motion information from periodic patterns, including the responses of orientation-selective and direction-selective neurons in early visual cortex. The aperture problem plays a significant role, as local motion detectors responding to grating contours can only signal motion perpendicular to the orientation of the bars, creating ambiguities that must be resolved through integration processes. Neural adaptation to sustained motion can also contribute to aftereffects and biases in perceived drift characteristics. Drifting grating illusions have been valuable tools for investigating motion processing mechanisms and have contributed substantially to computational models of how the brain analyzes visual motion.
