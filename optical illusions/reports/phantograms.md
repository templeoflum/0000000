## Abstract

Phantograms are specialized stereoscopic images designed to create a vivid illusion of three-dimensional objects appearing to float above or project from a flat printed surface when viewed from a specific vantage point. Unlike traditional stereograms that require binocular fusion of two separate images, phantograms present a single anamorphic projection that exploits monocular depth cues including perspective, shading, and size gradients to create compelling depth perception. The technique involves carefully distorting a photographed or rendered object according to precise geometric transformations, such that when the printed image is positioned at a particular angle relative to the viewer, the anamorphic distortion is perceptually corrected and the object appears to occupy real three-dimensional space. The illusion is particularly effective when the phantogram is mounted at a 45-degree angle and viewed from a predetermined location, where the visual system interprets the transformed perspective information as veridical depth structure. This phenomenon demonstrates the brain's powerful ability to reconstruct three-dimensional form from two-dimensional projections and the critical role of viewpoint geometry in visual perception. Phantograms have applications in advertising, art, and educational displays, providing accessible demonstrations of perspective geometry and depth perception without requiring special viewing equipment.
