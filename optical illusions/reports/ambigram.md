## Abstract

An ambigram is a typographic design that maintains readability when viewed from multiple orientations or perspectives, such as when rotated 180 degrees, reflected in a mirror, or viewed from different angles. This calligraphic art form exploits the perceptual system's ability to recognize letterforms even when they deviate substantially from canonical representations, relying on top-down processing and contextual expectations to resolve ambiguous visual information into coherent text. The creation of ambigrams requires careful manipulation of positive and negative space, symmetry principles, and stroke patterns to ensure that the same visual elements can be interpreted as different letters depending on viewing orientation. Ambigrams demonstrate fundamental principles of visual perception including figure-ground segregation, the role of expectation in pattern recognition, and the flexibility of the visual system in accommodating distorted or unconventional letterforms. They have been popularized in contemporary culture through logo design, book covers, and tattoo art, serving as both aesthetic objects and demonstrations of perceptual plasticity. The phenomenon reveals how the reading system prioritizes global word recognition over precise letter identification, allowing substantial shape variation while maintaining legibility across transformations.
