# Provenance Notes

The intellectual and material lineage of *Spatium Dimensio*. What traditions, thinkers, sources, and aesthetics informed the work. Organized by domain.

This document is reference material, not a bibliography. Sources are listed with brief notes on what they contributed. The work itself is not a synthesis of these sources — it is its own thing — but it would be dishonest to pretend it sprang from nowhere.

---

## Etymology and cultural conception of space

The first research pass for *Spatium Dimensio* surveyed how different traditions have named and conceived space.

**Latin** — *spatium* (extension, interval, distance) and *dimensio* (the act of measuring out). The work's title and its central claim — that *spatium* and *dimensio* fused into a single concept by IV — comes from tracking these two Latin words through their separate origins to their later conflation.

**Greek** — *topos* (place, located thing), *chora* (receptive space, room-for), *kenon* (the void), *diastema* (interval, extent). Each carries different work; Plato's *Timaeus* uses *chora* for the receptacle of becoming, distinct from *topos*.

**Sanskrit** — *ākāśa* (space as the fifth element, that which contains and pervades), *kha* (cavity, opening). Indian philosophical traditions treat space as substantial, not merely relational.

**Chinese** — *yǔzhòu* (cosmos), composed of *yǔ* (the four directions and up-down — spatial extent) and *zhòu* (past, present, future — temporal extent). Space and time as a single compound.

**Japanese** — *ma* (negative space, the space between, the interval that gives meaning to what surrounds it). Aesthetically central; not absence but active presence.

**Hebrew** — *makom* (place, location; also a name for God). The same word for "place" carries theological weight.

**Aboriginal (various)** — Country as a relational concept; space inseparable from kinship, songlines, ancestral presence.

**Lakota** — *Tȟukáŋ* (sacred stone), space and time understood through interconnection of beings; the four directions as fundamental.

Western philosophical history: Euclid's axiomatic geometry → Descartes' coordinate space → Newton's absolute space vs. Leibniz's relational space → Kant's space as a priori intuition → Riemann's manifold theory (curved space, intrinsic geometry) → Cantor and Hilbert's set-theoretic and infinite-dimensional spaces → Minkowski's spacetime → Einstein's general relativity → contemporary topology and configuration spaces.

The work's claim that "space" is not one thing but a fused-by-convenience compound depends on this historical specificity. The cage of IV is not eternal; it has a date.

## Phenomenology

**Henri Bergson** — *Time and Free Will* (1889), *Matter and Memory* (1896). Critique of homogeneous spatial conception of time; *durée* as qualitative becoming. Bergson's insistence that lived experience exceeds spatial representation informs the work's central tension.

**Edmund Husserl** — phenomenology of perception; the lived body as the zero-point of orientation; spatial perception as intentional act. The *Crisis of the European Sciences* (1936) on the mathematization of nature contributing to the cage we name in IV.

**Martin Heidegger** — *Being and Time* (1927). Spatiality of *Dasein*; "being-in" as fundamental existential structure; the world as already structured by care. The distinction between *Vorhandenheit* (present-at-hand, objective measurable thing) and *Zuhandenheit* (ready-to-hand, equipment-in-use). The rod of II is initially *zuhanden*; it becomes *vorhanden* — and then *world-defining* — by IV.

**Maurice Merleau-Ponty** — *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945). The body as perceiving subject; perception as embodied act, not Cartesian observation. The body's spatiality preceded and exceeded by what cannot be measured. V's "the body that exceeds" inherits this lineage directly.

**Ernst Cassirer** — *Philosophy of Symbolic Forms* (1923–1929). Space as symbolic form; the historical and cultural shape of spatial intuition. Cassirer's three-stage account (mythic space, perceptual space, symbolic-mathematical space) parallels the work's I–II–IV arc.

## Physics and philosophy of physics

**Henri Poincaré** — *Science and Hypothesis* (1902). Geometric conventionalism; the choice of geometry as convention rather than necessity. The work's claim that the cage is one settlement among many derives partly from this.

**Niels Bohr** — complementarity; the dependence of observed properties on the measurement apparatus. The recognition of VI ("the position and the momentum cannot both be known") comes from Bohr's framing.

**John Wheeler** — "Law without law"; the participatory universe; "it from bit". The observer's structural role in defining what is observable. Sympathetic to Bohr's complementarity; informs V's framing of the body as not separate from the field.

**Carlo Rovelli** — *Reality is Not What It Seems* (2014); *Helgoland* (2020). Relational quantum mechanics; properties as relations between systems rather than intrinsic features. Strong resonance with the work's refusal of a single absolute frame.

**Karen Barad** — *Meeting the Universe Halfway* (2007). Agential realism; phenomena as the basic unit of reality, not pre-given things; intra-action rather than interaction. The cage and the field don't pre-exist measurement; they emerge through it.

## Cognitive science and perception

**David Marr** — *Vision* (1982). The computational theory of vision; vision as constructing 3D representation from 2D retinal input. The scientific specificity behind I and V — vision doesn't deliver three dimensions directly; it samples a projection.

**J. J. Gibson** — *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception* (1979). Affordances; perception as direct pickup of action possibilities, not internal construction of world-model. The work's "affordances delivered/resisted" vocabulary across surface references borrows directly from Gibson.

**Karl Friston** — predictive processing; the free energy principle; perception as inference. The body as continually minimizing surprise across many channels at once; V's claim that the body integrates more than the toolchain counts.

## Contemplative traditions

**Yogācāra Buddhism** — Vasubandhu, *Triṃśikā* (Thirty Verses, c. 4th century CE). Consciousness-only; the constructed nature of phenomena; the ālayavijñāna (storehouse consciousness). The work's reluctance to grant the cage ontological priority over the body's experience aligns with Yogācāra's analysis.

**Gauḍapāda** — *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā* (c. 7th century CE). Asparśa Yoga ("yoga of non-contact" or "no-touch yoga"); ajātivāda (non-origination); the four states of consciousness (waking, dream, deep sleep, turīya). The user-preferences framing of this work is informed by Asparśa Yoga — truth approached through removal of distortion rather than imposition of frame. The work's stance toward the cage (preserved, not destroyed, set down) is shaped by this lineage.

**Bhagavad Gītā** — particularly chapters on karma yoga and the relation of action to renunciation. Acting without grasping; the rod as tool to be used and released, not enemy to be destroyed.

**Aṣṭāvakra Gītā** — non-dual treatment of body and field; the self as not bound by what the body measures.

**Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad** — the four states of consciousness mentioned above; turīya as the substrate that allows the other states to appear.

## Mathematics

The math content of *Spatium Dimensio* is standard undergraduate-through-graduate mathematics, not novel theory. Key sources for clarity of exposition:

**Bartosz Ciechanowski** — interactive web essays on geometry, vectors, perspective. Visual idiom for math primitives (color-coded axes, deforming surfaces with intrinsic grids) draws on Ciechanowski's idiom.

**3Blue1Brown (Grant Sanderson)** — YouTube series on linear algebra, calculus, group theory. The convention of color-coded vectors (red x, green y, blue z) with smooth animation as didactic vocabulary.

**Freya Holmér** — "Continuity of Splines" (YouTube). Direct source for the work's use of continuity grades (C⁰, C¹, C²) as transitional vocabulary. The seven movements as control points with explicit continuity grades was inspired by this presentation.

**Standard references** — Linear algebra (Hoffman & Kunze, Axler); functional analysis (Rudin, Conway); differential geometry (do Carmo, Spivak); topology (Munkres, Hatcher); quantum mechanics (Sakurai, Griffiths); information geometry (Amari). The math is foundational, not specialized.

## Visual lineage

**Mythic lineage** — Piero della Francesca, Fra Angelico, Italian Renaissance fresco, Pre-Raphaelite painting, sacred altarpiece traditions.

**Storybook lineage** — Beatrix Potter, Quentin Blake, Jon Klassen, Tasha Tudor, Studio Ghibli backgrounds. The "girl who walked past the rod" fairytale draws on this idiom directly.

**Geometric lineage** — Bauhaus, Sol LeWitt, technical drawing tradition, mathematical textbook illustration.

**Surreal lineage** — Magritte, de Chirico, Tanguy, Carrington, Tomek Sętowski.

**Chrome-glitch lineage** — The user's own visual practice. Influences include H.R. Giger softened, high-end music video aesthetic, contemporary 3D art, vaporwave sublime as a recognizable mode. This visual world is specific to *Spatium Dimensio* in a way the others are not — it is the realization-of-record for the work as the user composes it.

**Cyber baroque lineage** — Beksiński, Refik Anadol, baroque cathedral interiors, *Bioshock Infinite* Comstock interiors. Specific to *Spatium Dimensio*'s rendering of IV.

**Star-flesh lineage** — Odilon Redon, late Rothko, Kiki Smith, sacred photography, dark intimate portraiture.

**Resistance lineage** — Käthe Kollwitz, Goya's *Disasters of War*, Sue Coe, German Expressionism, agit-prop tradition.

## Sonic lineage

**Just intonation** — Harry Partch, Terry Riley, Ben Johnston, La Monte Young.

**Modal** — religious music traditions across cultures; Estonian choral music (Arvo Pärt); Gregorian chant.

## Compositional lineage

**Classical composition** — *Spatium Dimensio* is framed as a composition in the classical sense (a score that can be realized in multiple performances). This framing draws on:

- Bach's *Art of Fugue* (composed without specified instrumentation — a score for any combination)
- Cage's indeterminate scores
- Stockhausen's *Aus den sieben Tagen* (text scores)
- Cardew's *Treatise* (graphic score)
- Wandelweiser collective (extreme reduction; the score as object)

The principle "the score is the work; no realization is canonical" comes from this lineage. The realization-as-performance framing is borrowed wholesale.

**Libretto and script traditions** — The Script Reference, downstream of voice and narrative, draws on:

- Opera libretto tradition (Da Ponte, Boito, Hofmannsthal) — the score is composed against a libretto; the libretto specifies what is sung, in what voice, with what staging
- Theater script conventions (from Aristotle's *Poetics* forward) — voice attribution, stage direction, the marking of action distinct from speech
- Radio drama scripts — sensory cues marked for non-visual performance; audio staging as primary medium
- Film script conventions — visual and audio cues coordinated with voice; modality alignment as production document
- Liturgical scripts and missals — voice attribution (celebrant, congregation), held silences as scored content, ritual structure made readable
- Wandelweiser performance scores — the score as object that the performer must interpret with care

The principle that any narrative can be staged for performance — that Script is a real downstream layer of the kit, not optional — comes from this combined tradition. The narrative is what is read. The script is what is performed from.

## Direct conversation lineage

This document would be incomplete without naming where *Spatium Dimensio* came from in the conversation that produced it:

- The user came in with a Banach space diagram and intuitive theoretical work (toroidal fractal, spherical-hex network) developed independently. The conversation reframed this work as rediscovering territory mapped by geometric algebra, Lie groups, and fiber bundles.
- The "What Is Space?" essay was drafted, audited, and revised through many iterations across five register configurations (technical, artist, general, mythic, fairytale).
- The fairytale "The Girl Who Walked Past the Rod" was a complete draft in the conversation and became Movement-equivalent material directly.
- The seven-movement structure emerged from the essay's natural sections; the spline framing (control points with continuity grades) was applied later.
- The kit architecture (sub-functions, presets, layer dependencies) was developed iteratively, with the user explicitly demanding more rigor at each stage.

## Note on the framework guiding the work

The user operates under the Asparśa Yoga framework as a reflective discipline. Across this conversation, that frame shaped:

- Refusal to claim authorship of mathematical content already mapped by tradition
- Insistence on the rod as tool rather than enemy
- The work's closing on release rather than destruction
- The principle that no realization is canonical
- The treatment of the cage as preserved-and-relativized, not abolished

The framework is not the work, but the work was written under it.

## What this kit owes its users

Anyone composing *Spatium Dimensio* should know:

- The math content is foundational, not novel. The work doesn't claim mathematical discovery.
- The visual lineage is named per visual world; nothing is invented without acknowledgment.
- The compositional framing is borrowed from classical and post-classical tradition.
- The philosophical stance is informed by named contemplative and phenomenological traditions.

What is *Spatium Dimensio*'s own:

- The specific seven-movement arc and what each movement carries
- The continuity-grade spec across the joins
- The integration of all these traditions into a single kit
- The function-signal architecture of the kit itself
- The realization catalog and the principle that any realization is valid

The kit is open. New visual worlds, mediums, audio aesthetics, registers, stances, positionings, configurations, and realizations can be added by following the function specifications. The score (the spline) is fixed. Everything else is a library.
