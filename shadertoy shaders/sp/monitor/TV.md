# Heart Monitor

**Detach TV** beneath the docked picture (or Command-T) opens a little 320 × 250 desktop television. It has no operating-system title bar. The silver enclosure, inset black screen surround and lower grille take their cues from Sony's compact KV-4P1; the product carries Heart branding. See TV-DESIGN.md for the reference.

- Drag the silver enclosure or unused lower fascia to move it.
- Double-click the picture or enclosure to enlarge to 640 × 430. Double-click again to restore the exact previous small frame.
- Drag the bottom-right diagonal grip to resize freely (minimum 280 × 220).
- **Dock** returns the picture to Heart. Command-T also docks it.
- **Pin ● / Pin ○** toggles always-on-top. The TV starts pinned.
- **Fit / 1:1** shows the whole picture or actual source pixels, which may crop in a small window.
- After sleep, **Resume** explicitly restarts the monitor. Motion remains held separately.

The readout stays in a dark recessed strip below the unfiltered picture. A layered charcoal enclosure, inset screen trim and feet surround a warm brushed aluminum fascia. Engraved FIT/DOCK/PIN labels sit above molded raised caps with separate black sockets, side faces and bevels; pressing a cap visibly lowers it. The pin state uses a small white lens on the metal. Hardware scales with the expanded window. Tiny mode shows live/stale/paused state and FPS. Larger mode adds GPU usage and source resolution; the tooltip supplies the fuller reading. Controls have at least 26-point-high hit areas; Resume joins the silver fascia when suspended. The maker plate reads BOUSTROPHEDON INSTRUMENT CO. and uses the canonical three-row directional mark. At minimum size with Resume visible, the mark alone hides to leave all four controls clear; product and maker names remain. Heart starts docked and detaches into small mode. Small and expanded rectangles are saved independently across launches, including manual movement and resizing; expansion starts at 640 × 430 only without a saved expanded frame. Fully offscreen rectangles return to an available display; reachable geometry stays unchanged. Old titled-window autosave is not used.

The enclosure is static AppKit drawing outside the video. The original monitor panel, Metal view, command queue and Syphon receiver move together. There is one rendering path and no image copying, second receiver, CRT filter or source-resolution change. Fit preserves source aspect ratio. Sleep and pointer-release safety remain in effect; detachment never starts suspended work or creates a wake assertion.

Build and hidden AppKit checks cover same-view detach/dock, ordering and visibility, key-capable TV window, compact layout mode, pinning, enlargement and exact restoration, ignored enlargement while docked, and preserved suspension. Existing session tests pass. Hidden tests use a fresh isolated preference suite, delete it afterward, reject native named-window autosave and disable restoration. They exercise independent geometry recall, offscreen recovery, same-button reparenting across repeated detach/dock, retained actions, focus styling and unchanged window geometry. Detached keyboard focus uses a warm cap outline rather than the standard blue focus ring. The offline render_tv.m harness renders small, large, pressed and minimum-size Resume views with a dummy grid specimen; it creates no Metal device, Syphon receiver, visible window or user preference writes. The specimen exists only in this harness. Live gesture delivery and performance require integration checks in the running app.
